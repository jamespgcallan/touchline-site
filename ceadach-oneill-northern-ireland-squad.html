Céadach O’Neill Named in Northern Ireland Squad Amid Republic Interest

The Arsenal teenager and Everton’s Braiden Graham are both in Michael O’Neill’s Nations League squad as the FAI monitor their international futures.

James Callan15 Sep 20265 min read

Céadach O’Neill has been named in Northern Ireland’s squad for their upcoming UEFA Nations League games, and the FAI’s hopes of bringing the Arsenal teenager into the Republic of Ireland setup could soon get a lot more complicated.

The 18-year-old already has two senior Northern Ireland caps after appearing against Guinea and France in June. Everton forward Braiden Graham is in the squad too, another highly rated teenager who has been linked with a possible switch to the Republic.

So yes, two exciting young attackers at Arsenal and Everton have somehow led us straight into FIFA eligibility regulations. There are easier ways to spend an international window.

Céadach’s situation is the one to watch most closely.

His two Northern Ireland appearances came in friendlies, so the door to a future Republic switch is still open. If he plays once in the Nations League, though, he would reach three senior appearances and any future change of association would then come with FIFA’s three-year waiting period, provided he still met the rest of the criteria.

If he plays twice, he would move beyond the three-cap limit for that route.

In normal football language, sitting on the bench changes nothing. One appearance makes things awkward. Two could basically finish the conversation.

That means a 10-minute cameo at the end of a Nations League game could end up being far more important than most 10-minute cameos ever deserve to be.

Michael O’Neill has already made it clear that he won’t be putting Céadach on the pitch just to stop the Republic getting him. The Northern Ireland manager recently met the teenager in London and said the subject of switching associations didn’t even come up.

“Honestly? We didn’t speak about it. We don’t need to speak about it.”

And, to be fair, Céadach hasn’t exactly been dragged into the squad from nowhere.

He scored 18 goals across Arsenal’s academy sides last season and has already spent time around Mikel Arteta’s first-team group, including during pre-season. Before moving to Arsenal in 2024, he came through Linfield and the Irish FA Academy, making his senior Linfield debut at just 15.

Graham’s route hasn’t been massively different.

He became Linfield’s youngest ever senior player at 15 years and 137 days, also came through the Irish FA Academy and joined Everton in 2024. Last season he scored 22 goals in 31 appearances for Everton’s Under-21s and he has already started popping up in David Moyes’ Premier League matchday squads.

He was an unused substitute against Tottenham at the weekend.

At 18, sitting on a Premier League bench while everyone else is trying to work out what they’re doing with their life is probably not the worst position to be in.

Graham was also involved with Northern Ireland in June but didn’t actually get onto the pitch. He was ready to come on against France in added time before the ball decided it simply wasn’t going out of play.

You couldn’t really script a more football way to postpone an international debut.

His situation is therefore a little less urgent than Céadach’s, but having both teenagers in the same competitive squad is still worth watching. The FAI have been keeping tabs on Irish-eligible players coming through in Britain, while Northern Ireland have been happy to get their own younger players into the senior environment early.

Michael O’Neill’s latest squad has an average age of 24, includes four teenagers and has 17 players under 25. Céadach and Graham aren’t just there to fill a couple of seats on the plane.

Northern Ireland start away to Georgia on 25 September before hosting Hungary three days later, with Ukraine and Georgia to follow in October.

By then, we might know a lot more about where both players’ international futures are heading.

For the Republic, this could be one of those rare weeks where the Northern Ireland substitution board is worth watching nearly as closely as the score.
