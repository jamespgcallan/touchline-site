Michael Noonan Set for Nottingham Forest Move From Shamrock Rovers

The 18-year-old could leave for compensation just months after Rovers accepted a €1.8 million bid from Hoffenheim.

James Callan14 Sep 20264 min read

Michael Noonan is reportedly heading to Nottingham Forest in January, just eight months after Shamrock Rovers accepted a €1.8 million offer from Hoffenheim for the teenage striker.

The 18-year-old is due to undergo a medical with Forest on Wednesday ahead of a move when his Rovers contract expires at the end of the League of Ireland season. Neither club has confirmed an agreement, but the Irish Sun reports that Forest have won the race for his signature and that Rovers will instead receive compensation for the move.

It’s some change from where things stood in January. Hoffenheim had two approaches rejected before Rovers accepted an upfront €1.8 million bid, only for Noonan to decide against the move to Germany. Ajax were later in advanced talks, while Celtic, Chelsea and Forest have also been linked with him. For an 18-year-old with barely two full seasons of senior football behind him, it’s not exactly a bad list of missed calls.

Noonan’s time at Tallaght has been short, but a lot has happened in it. He arrived from St Patrick’s Athletic at 16 in January 2025, already one of the standout players of his age in Ireland, after becoming Pat’s youngest-ever starter at 15 years and nine months. Rovers weren’t signing him for the Under-20s either. Stephen Bradley said from the beginning that he expected Noonan to be around his first team.

“Michael is one of the best young players in the country and, as well as producing our own, we got to recruit the best as well so Michael falls into that category.”

A month later, Noonan scored away to Molde on his European debut and became the youngest goalscorer in UEFA Conference League history at 16 years and 197 days. He was named Rovers’ Young Player of the Year after helping them win the 2025 Premier Division and has followed that with seven league goals in 28 appearances this season.

That route is an important part of how Rovers have operated in recent years. Roadstone has produced players of its own, but Bradley’s side have also been willing to bring in elite teenagers from elsewhere and give them senior minutes earlier than they might get abroad. Noonan came from St Pat’s looking for that opportunity and, less than two years later, Premier League clubs are queuing up for him.

Victor Ozhianvuna offers a slightly different version of the same story. He joined Rovers at six and will move to Arsenal in January 2027 after a deal understood to guarantee the club around €2 million, with add-ons and a reported sell-on clause on top. Noonan came much later, went almost immediately into Bradley’s first-team plans and could now leave months after Rovers had €1.8 million agreed for him.

Rovers couldn’t force him to accept Hoffenheim, of course, and having one of the country’s best young forwards available while trying to win another league title was hardly a terrible consolation prize. Still, it’s a good example of how quickly the numbers can move for League of Ireland clubs once a young player gets close to the end of his contract.

Those numbers carry more weight at Tallaght than they once did. Rovers posted a €1.18 million after-tax profit for 2024 after a €2.37 million loss the previous year, with European income and the initial €450,000 received from Como for Naj Razi among the factors behind the turnaround. Player development isn’t replacing the business of winning trophies either. Rovers claimed a record 22nd league title in 2025, their fifth in six seasons.

They’re chasing another one now, although Friday night’s 2-0 defeat to St Patrick’s Athletic has made the run-in considerably less comfortable. Rovers remain three points clear after 30 games, with Pat’s able to go top on goal difference if they beat Dundalk on Monday night.

Noonan still has six league games left with Rovers before any move can happen, starting with Waterford at Tallaght Stadium on Friday night.

Before that, if Monday’s reports are right, he’ll be in Nottingham for his medical on Wednesday. Back in January, the destination was Germany and €1.8 million was on the table. Football has managed to rewrite both parts before the year is even out.

If this one landed, the newsletter’s where the rest of it lives — two or three pieces a week, no noise.
