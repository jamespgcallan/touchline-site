Shamrock Rovers Teen Jayden Marshall Signs Three-Year Como Deal

The 16-year-old centre-back has joined the Serie A club after a rapid rise through Shamrock Rovers and the Republic of Ireland underage setup.

James Callan22 Sep 20264 min read

Shamrock Rovers teenager Jayden Marshall has signed a three-year deal with Serie A side Como, taking the 16-year-old centre-back from Tallaght to Italy after a big couple of years in Irish academy football.

The fee is undisclosed, with the move confirmed on Tuesday afternoon. There had already been a fairly decent clue a few days earlier when the FAI named its Republic of Ireland Under-17 squad and listed Marshall as a Como 1907 player. The official announcement was still to come, but somebody had clearly updated the team sheet before the rest of us caught up.

Marshall has packed quite a lot into a short space of time. The Tallaght native started with Crumlin United before joining Shelbourne and then Shamrock Rovers, where he was quickly pushed above his own age group. By early 2025 he was playing for Ireland at Under-16 level, while Rovers had him involved with their Under-20s despite him only turning 16 in January.

Stephen Bradley had also started bringing him around the senior squad. Marshall featured in Rovers’ 1-0 friendly win over Hibernian at Tallaght Stadium in June, giving him a first taste of playing alongside senior professionals and giving the club a chance to see how comfortable he looked when the pace and physicality went up another level.

He leaves before establishing himself in the Premier Division first team, but clubs abroad are making these calls earlier and earlier. Rovers have already seen Josh O’Dwyer agree a move to Red Bull Salzburg after reaching Under-20 football at 15, while goalkeeper Alex Noonan joined Ipswich Town this month after breaking into the senior setup.

For a club that has spent the last few years being talked about mainly in terms of league titles, Europe and Stephen Bradley’s decade in charge, the academy has become another part of what Rovers are now known for. Roadstone is producing players who are getting close enough to senior football for clubs abroad to make their move before the rest of us have really had time to learn their names.

Como have clearly been watching Ireland too. Naj Razi left Rovers for the Italian club in 2024, while Bohemians striker Josh Harpur followed earlier this month. Harpur has gone into Como’s Primavera setup, which is probably a better guide to Marshall’s next step than imagining he is about to walk straight into Cesc Fàbregas’ Serie A back four.

The club have put a lot of work into the level below the first team, with specialist coaching, fitness, nutrition and individual development all part of how younger players are brought through. Marshall arrives as exactly that: a highly-rated Irish centre-back with underage international experience, some exposure around a senior side and a lot still to learn.

Razi’s spell at Como is also a reminder of how difficult the next bit can be. He returned to Shamrock Rovers this year without making a Serie A appearance. Moving abroad at 16 or 17 can open a very big door, but there is still a lot of football to play once you get through it.

For now, Marshall’s next job is with Ireland. He is part of Keith O’Halloran’s Under-17 squad for games against England, Georgia, Poland and Czechia, beginning at St George’s Park on Wednesday.

A year ago, he was still coming through the academy system in Dublin.

Now the Ireland team sheet says Como beside his name.

If this one landed, the newsletter’s where the rest of it lives — two or three pieces a week, no noise.
