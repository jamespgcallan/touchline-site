Cristiano Ronaldo Leaves Portugal Camp After Jorge Jesus Row

The 41-year-old has walked out of Portugal’s camp after being left unused against Norway, with his role under Jorge Jesus suddenly looking far less certain.

James Callan1 Oct 20267 min read

Cristiano Ronaldo has left Portugal’s national-team camp in Copenhagen, promised supporters he will eventually tell them “the truth”, and left Jorge Jesus dealing with the first proper headache of his time as Portugal manager.

The Portuguese Football Federation confirmed Ronaldo’s departure on Wednesday night. He will miss Portugal’s Nations League meeting with Denmark, while the rest of the squad continue preparations without him. Ronaldo said his decision came after Jesus’s press conference and a conversation with FPF president Pedro Proença, but did not explain what had pushed him to leave.

“In due course, I will tell all Portuguese people the truth about the reasons that led to my departure.”

That explanation is still to come. Ronaldo has not announced his retirement from international football, the federation has not said he was sent home, and Jesus has denied reports that his captain refused to train. There is enough going on here without filling in the blanks for him.

The awkwardness started three days earlier in Oslo, when Jesus left Ronaldo out of the starting XI against Norway. He had started the opening win over Wales, but this time Gonçalo Ramos got the nod. Jesus later said he had considered bringing Ronaldo on for the final half-hour before deciding the game did not need him.

Portugal won 2-1. Ramos scored the winner.

For Ronaldo, spending a full competitive international on the bench is almost an event in itself. It had not happened since Portugal faced Switzerland at Euro 2008, when the team had already qualified from their group. Eighteen years later, he watched the whole match in Oslo and went down the tunnel after the final whistle rather than joining his team-mates as they acknowledged the travelling support.

From there, what could have stayed as a selection story started to grow legs.

Ronaldo worked separately from the group over the next two days despite not being injured, while questions about his relationship with Jesus followed Portugal to Copenhagen. By Wednesday, the manager was already being asked whether his captain had refused to train. Jesus denied it, said the pair had spoken about Ronaldo’s role and told reporters he expected him to join the evening session.

He never did.

Ronaldo left Parken Stadium and soon confirmed he was leaving the camp altogether. Reuters later reported, citing Marca, that Jesus and Proença went to Copenhagen airport in an attempt to persuade him to stay before he flew to Madrid. The FPF has not publicly confirmed that account.

That sequence is why this has become more than a row over one night on the bench.

Jesus knows Ronaldo well. He managed him at Al-Nassr before taking the Portugal job, so this is not a new coach learning for the first time how much attention follows every decision involving him. He also arrived with a fairly clear idea of what he wanted from the squad: places had to be earned, and reputation would not settle every selection call.

Against Norway, he followed through on that.

He picked Ramos. Portugal won. Ramos scored. Ronaldo stayed on the bench.

That is the football part of the story, and it is probably the bit Portugal will care most about once the noise around the camp settles. Ronaldo can still score, still occupy defenders and still offer something very few international sides have. The difficulty now is working out how that fits into a team that is beginning another cycle and no longer has to be built around him every week.

Portugal have been here before. Fernando Santos dropped Ronaldo during the knockout stages of the 2022 World Cup and Ramos responded with a hat-trick against Switzerland. Ronaldo later returned to the centre of the side and remained a major part of Roberto Martínez’s plans, including Portugal’s Nations League triumph in 2025.

This summer’s World Cup moved things on again. Ronaldo said afterwards that it had been his last World Cup, but he did not step away from international football. That leaves Portugal in a slightly awkward middle ground: planning for what comes next while their most famous player still wants to be part of what comes now.

There is room for both things to be true. Ronaldo has 234 caps and 146 international goals. He does not need his value to Portugal explained to him, Jesus or anybody else. But international careers rarely end at the exact point a player stops being useful. More often, the role changes first.

That is the bit Jesus is now trying to manage.

Portugal face Denmark in Copenhagen on Thursday night without Ronaldo. He has promised his side of the story, and there will hardly be a shortage of people waiting to hear it.

Until then, Jesus has a match to manage and Ronaldo has left the city.

For once, Portugal’s biggest Ronaldo question is not whether he starts.

It is whether he comes back.

If this one landed, the newsletter’s where the rest of it lives — two or three pieces a week, no noise.
