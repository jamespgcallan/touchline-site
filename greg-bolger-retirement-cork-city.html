Greg Bolger to Retire After Almost 20 Years in League of Ireland

The 38-year-old will finish his career at Turner’s Cross on Friday, nearly 20 years after making his League of Ireland debut against Cork City.

James Callan5 Oct 20264 min read

Greg Bolger will retire from professional football after Cork City face Finn Harps at Turner’s Cross on Friday night, bringing an almost 20-year League of Ireland career to an end.

There is a nice symmetry to where it finishes. Bolger made his league debut for UCD against Cork City in March 2007 and, 19 years later, his final appearance will come in a Cork shirt at Turner’s Cross. City will collect the First Division trophy afterwards, so the night already had the makings of a celebration before Bolger gave supporters another reason to stick around.

The 38-year-old announced his decision on Sunday evening, saying the venue makes the farewell particularly special.

“I couldn’t be happier that the final game of my career will be at Turner’s Cross, a place that has given me some of my fondest memories.”

Cork described him as a “true Cork City Football Club and League of Ireland legend”. The word gets a decent workout in football, but Bolger has built a stronger case for it than most.

His career has taken him from UCD to Dundalk, St Patrick’s Athletic, Cork City, Shamrock Rovers and Sligo Rovers. There was even a spell at Sporting Fingal in 2011, although the club folded before he managed a competitive appearance. Spending nearly two decades in the League of Ireland was always going to produce at least one story like that.

Plenty of better ones followed. Bolger won the Premier Division with St Pat’s in 2013, was part of the Cork side that completed a league and FAI Cup double in 2017 and won another title with Shamrock Rovers in 2020. His career haul stands at three Premier Division titles, three First Division titles and four FAI Cups.

The list of clubs says plenty as well. Bolger was never the player a team was built around or the name filling highlight compilations, but managers kept trusting him and successful sides kept finding room for him. That is a useful way of measuring a midfielder who has managed to remain relevant in the league from his early 20s right through to 38.

Cork eventually became home again. He returned from Sligo Rovers before the 2024 season and helped City win promotion at the first attempt. After relegation from the Premier Division last year, Bolger stayed and has now been part of another promotion, with City wrapping up the 2026 First Division title with six games remaining.

Friday will not be the end of his involvement at the club either. Bolger is already working within Cork City’s academy and joined the Under-20 coaching staff as part of his latest contract, which feels like a fairly natural next stop for somebody who has spent most of his adult life inside League of Ireland dressing rooms.

For now, there is still one game left.

Cork host Finn Harps on Friday before getting their hands on the First Division trophy, while Bolger gets one final night at a ground where he won some of the biggest trophies of his career.

His first League of Ireland game came against Cork City in March 2007. His last will come for them.

Nineteen years is a long way around to end up back at Turner’s Cross, but it feels about right.
