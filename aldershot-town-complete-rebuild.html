Aldershot Town's Complete Rebuild: 21 Signings, 25 Departures

Most clubs call five or six new players a rebuild. Scott Davies arrived at Aldershot Town and found himself replacing practically everyone except the ground keeper.

James Callan 27 Aug 2026 7 min read

Football has become very generous with the word rebuild.

A club moves on four or five players, signs a new striker, changes the manager and before long everyone is talking about year one of a three-year plan.

Aldershot Town have gone a fair bit further.

Scott Davies has brought in 21 new players this summer. By July, 25 players from last season's group had left. This wasn't a case of adding a few better players around an existing core. There basically wasn't much of a core left.

Davies himself admits it wasn't how he expected his first summer in charge to go.

"We've released the 25th player from last year's group yesterday, so it's been a complete rebuild. Was it my plan? No. Was it my intention? No. But I felt like it was needed. We've got 21 new players now and I love every single one of them."

The opening weekend showed exactly what that meant. Aldershot started away to Boston United with 11 competitive debutants. Every single player in the starting XI was new.

They won 1-0.

John Gilbert, Davies' first signing, scored the winner. A week later the exact same XI started against Hartlepool at the Recreation Ground and lost 1-0, with Marcus Dinanga missing a late penalty.

Two games won't tell us whether this will work. They do tell us just how unusual this summer has been.

Wembley Only Told Half The Story

The strange bit is that Aldershot were celebrating at Wembley only 15 months ago.

More than 18,000 Shots supporters travelled there in May 2025 and watched Tommy Widdrington's side beat Spennymoor Town 3-0 in the FA Trophy final. Jack Barham, Dan Ellison and Josh Barrett scored. For Aldershot Town, it was their first FA Trophy and the phoenix club's first appearance at Wembley.

It looked like a day you could build from.

The league table was saying something else.

Aldershot had finished eighth in 2023-24, slipped to 16th the following year and then finished 20th last season. They won 13 of 46 league games, lost 26 and conceded 87 goals, finishing only four points above the relegation places.

Widdrington resigned in October and John Coleman came in, but his spell ended badly. Aldershot lost ten of his final 12 games and after defeat at Brackley, Coleman didn't exactly hold back.

"I've been kidded thinking that I've got players who are good enough. The brutal fact is that the players aren't good enough."

Ten days before leaving, owner Deane Wood had said Coleman would "definitely" be in charge the following season.

Football has never been particularly kind to the word definitely.

Davies arrived eight days later. He already knew the club well, having made more than 90 appearances across three spells and scoring the goal against Torquay that helped confirm promotion back to the Football League in 2008.

This time, though, he wasn't coming back to join a team.

He was being asked to build one.

And He Had To Do It For Less Money

Results were only half the problem.

Wood took control in January and later said Aldershot had previously been carrying around £2 million in debt, with restructuring bringing that figure closer to £1 million.

By April, he was warning that the club could lose somewhere between £600,000 and £800,000 during the year. The playing budget for this season was also being cut by roughly £200,000, leaving it at around 85 per cent of what it had been.

Even after that, Wood expected another loss of about £500,000.

At one point, he revealed that the electricity bill at the Recreation Ground alone was costing roughly £4,500 a month.

That puts the 21 signings in a very different light.

Davies wasn't handed a big budget and told to enjoy himself. He was being asked to replace most of a team that had finished 20th while also making the new one cheaper.

He explains the problem in a way that probably sounds familiar to anyone who has ever tried to put a squad together.

"You've got a budget at the beginning and you feel rich. Then all of a sudden, when you start spending it, you feel like, 'Oh, actually, I'm running out of money here and I still need to bring in three or four players.' It soon goes, especially when you start to negotiate with players. You have to juggle, so it's not easy. It's definitely difficult, but you've got to be good at maths, which hopefully I've done. I've stuck to the budget."

That is probably the clearest way of understanding this summer.

Twenty-one signings sounds mad until you remember they all still have to fit inside one budget.

So Who Do You Sign When You Need Everyone?

Davies has understandably gone back to players he already knows.

Gilbert had played under him at Slough Town before scoring ten league goals for Maidstone last season. Kiki Oshilaja also worked with Davies at Slough. Jacob Borgnis had a loan spell there. Tyrese Dyce played under him for two seasons.

That isn't laziness. It is probably common sense.

When nearly every player walking into the dressing room is new, knowing what someone is like from Monday to Friday matters. You want to know who trains properly, who can deal with a bad result and who you can trust when the weather turns, the pitches get heavier and the National League starts looking a lot less glamorous in November.

Davies has also mixed that familiarity with players who already know the level. Nick Hayes arrived with more than 100 National League appearances. Michee Efete has played in the EFL and Scottish Premiership. Marcus Dinanga scored 19 National League goals for Gateshead before moving into the Football League with Bromley.

Then there are players for whom Aldershot represents a proper chance to go full-time.

"They told me what the budget was when they offered me the job, and it was a lot more than I've been used to. When you speak to players and you can offer them full-time football, they all can quit their jobs. I was able to give pretty much every single player in the squad more than I've ever given anyone before. You're changing people's lives, right? First opportunity going to pro football."

That gives the rebuild a more human side.

Signing number 17 can start to look like just another club graphic on social media. For the player, it might mean leaving a job and becoming a full-time footballer for the first time.

Aldershot Have Been Here Before, Just Not Like This

There is obviously a history here too.

The original Aldershot FC folded during the 1991-92 Football League season. Aldershot Town was formed weeks later and started again in Isthmian League Division Three, with 1,493 people attending the first league game against Clapton.

It took 16 years and five promotions to get back into the Football League.

Then, in 2013, the new club entered administration after relegation from League Two, with debts reported at around £1.4 million and players going unpaid.

Nobody is saying Aldershot are in that position now. They aren't.

But supporters here hardly need reminding what happens when the numbers stop adding up.

That is why this summer is about more than 21 transfer announcements. It is about a manager trying to improve a team while spending less, an owner trying to get the club back onto firmer financial ground and a dressing room being built almost from zero.

So far, that new team has won one and lost one.

There will probably be plenty more good days and bad days before anyone really knows whether Davies has got it right.

Most clubs call five or six new signings a rebuild.

Aldershot signed 21 and started the season with an entirely new XI.

For once, the word probably fits.
