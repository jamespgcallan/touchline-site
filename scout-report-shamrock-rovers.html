Shamrock Rovers

A squad audit built around one question: can Rovers find a midfielder who does the defensive work around Matt Healy rather than simply trying to replace him?

Overview

Rovers do not need a rebuild.

They already have enough quality to control most games in Ireland. The midfield gap is more specific. Matt Healy is heavily involved in how Rovers keep the ball, averaging almost 107 touches and 85 completed passes per 90 at 90% accuracy. He is much closer to a controller than an out-and-out ball-winner.

Recruitment objective

Find a No.6 who adds defensive power without taking away from the technical level of the midfield.

  • Protect the back three
  • Defend transitions
  • Win more duels
  • Cover larger spaces
  • Stay reliable enough on the ball to play for Rovers

Why it matters

The imbalance shows most when games become stretched, especially in Europe. The UK market should form a bigger part of the search because Rovers can offer young players senior football, trophies and Europe.

Current benchmark

What is missing?

The gap becomes obvious once you look at Healy's full profile. He is in the 99th percentile for touches but only the 16th percentile for defensive contributions among midfielders. Rovers already have the midfielder who wants the ball. They need one who helps get it back.

Playing model

The role beside Healy

In possession

Healy is already one of the main reference points in Rovers' build-up. The next midfielder does not need to be another deep playmaker. Healy can already do that job. The new No.6 needs to be good enough technically that Rovers can still play through him when pressed.

Healy, Byrne and Watts already give Rovers plenty of progression. The ideal signing wins the ball and gets Rovers back into their passing game quickly.

Final third

  • Win second balls
  • Stop counters
  • Recycle possession
  • Keep Rovers higher up the pitch

Out of possession

Healy averages 1.71 tackles, 1.00 interception and 4.51 defensive contributions per 90. Those are solid numbers, but not dominant defensive numbers. The new signing should be the more aggressive defender in the partnership.

  • Get across the pitch quickly
  • Stop counters before they develop
  • Win ground duels
  • Close central passing lanes
  • Recover loose balls
  • Immediately find Healy, Byrne or Watts

Extra size would help too. Healy wins 50% of his aerial duels, so a stronger aerial presence would improve the midfield profile, although it is a bonus rather than the main requirement.

Player fit

The leading options

Aodh Dervin · Dundalk

Dervin is probably the clearest domestic value option. He already understands the league and plays naturally as a defensive midfielder. His profile is heavily weighted towards defensive work, ranking in the 92nd percentile for defensive contributions among comparable midfielders.

Verdict: Strong value target.

Jamie Andrews · Solihull Moors

Andrews is the most interesting development option from outside Ireland. He ranks around the 94th percentile for defensive contributions and 91st percentile for aerial duels won within his comparison group. At 23, there is genuine development and resale potential.

Verdict: High-upside UK target.

Value assessment

Spend the money on the contract, not the fee.

The money should go into wages and contract structure rather than unnecessary transfer fees. Dervin and McInroy are particularly attractive because both are listed in the report with contracts ending in November 2026.

Add the profile Rovers do not already have.
