Christian Eriksen Leaves Wolfsburg After Contract Terminated Early

The Denmark international will continue his rehabilitation closer to his family after bringing his eventful spell in Germany to an early end.

James Callan24 Sep 20265 min read

Christian Eriksen has left Wolfsburg after agreeing to terminate his contract nine months early, with the Denmark international choosing to remain at home while he continues his rehabilitation.

The 34-year-old was contracted until 30 June 2027, but Wolfsburg confirmed on Wednesday that they had accepted his request to leave. Eriksen has been completing an individual rehabilitation programme in Denmark since July following his collapse during a friendly against Ukraine on 7 June, and being close to his family has understandably become more important than getting back to Germany.

Eriksen later explained that the incident was different from the cardiac arrest he suffered against Finland at Euro 2020 in 2021. His implanted cardioverter-defibrillator activated during the Ukraine match before he underwent further medical checks and returned home.

Explaining his decision to leave Wolfsburg, Eriksen said:

“At the moment, it is important for me to be close to my family in Denmark. I’m pleased that we were able to find a solution together.”

Wolfsburg were happy to leave that call with him. Managing director for sport Dieter Hecking said the club wanted Eriksen to have control over what happened next.

“For us, it was clear that the decision about his future had to be Christian’s. We wanted to give him that freedom in this particular situation.”

It brings to an end a Wolfsburg career that only lasted a year, although there was enough packed into those 12 months to make it feel considerably longer.

Eriksen arrived as a free agent in September 2025 after leaving Manchester United. Wolfsburg had spent that summer reshaping their squad, reducing its size and bringing in a number of younger players, and saw Eriksen as someone who could provide a bit of experience alongside captain Maximilian Arnold.

It wasn't simply a case of seeing a famous name available on a free transfer and working out the details afterwards. Sporting director Sebastian Schindzielorz specifically spoke about Eriksen helping the club's younger players, which made sense for someone who had already played for Ajax, Tottenham, Inter Milan and Manchester United and had accumulated more than 300 Premier League appearances.

What followed was a strange season for everyone involved.

Eriksen finished it with 34 appearances across the Bundesliga, DFB-Pokal and relegation play-offs, scoring three goals and providing ten assists. Wolfsburg, meanwhile, ended it in the 2. Bundesliga.

Their play-off defeat to Paderborn in May brought 29 consecutive seasons of top-flight football to an end and delivered the first Bundesliga relegation in the club's history. This was, after all, a club that won the German title in 2009, lifted the DFB-Pokal in 2015 and had become a fairly permanent part of the Bundesliga furniture. A relegation play-off defeat probably wasn't what anyone had in mind when Eriksen was brought in to add a little stability.

His departure now has little to do with any of that. Wolfsburg are already trying to get back up, while Eriksen has something rather more important to concentrate on.

There has been no retirement announcement and nothing in Wednesday's statement says his playing career is finished. At 34, Denmark's record appearance holder has simply chosen not to put a deadline on what happens next.

Wolfsburg can spend the coming months worrying about getting back to the Bundesliga. Eriksen, for once, doesn't need to worry about the football calendar.
